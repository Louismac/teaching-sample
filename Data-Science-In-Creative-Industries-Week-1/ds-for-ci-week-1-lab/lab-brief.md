# Missing Datasets Task 

← [Week 1 overview](../README.md) · [All teaching samples](../../README.md)

Taking lead from [Mimi Onuoha's Repository of Missing Datasets](https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/missing-datasets)

## In groups of 4-5

* Think of (at least) one dataset that could be added to the repository of missing datasets.

* Why do you think this dataset should exist?

* Why do you think it doesn't exist?

* If available, how are datasets presented and how do we open and start looking at them? * If not, what considerations would arise (in terms of access and presentation) for necessary data currently missing?

## You might consider

* Data which is challenging to collect

* Data which is held across fragmented platforms or hard to unify / collate (e.g. gig ecomony workers)

* Historical biases that meant data was collected at all

* Data collection that faced legal issues to collect (e.g. certain LGBTQ issues at given times or countries)

* Where data is challenging to record (e.g. things that are underreported)

# Dear Data Task

We will now create a physical artifact, drawing or record a performance that visually or artistically represents a specific set of data.

## Part 1: Introduction and Brainstorming (30 minutes)

### Introduction (10 minutes):
Intro to  "Dear Data" project (personal data and how it can be represented creatively!)

### Group Formation and Brainstorming (20 minutes):
* Form groups of 2-3 students.

* Each group selects a theme for their data collection (e.g., daily water intake, number of steps walked, hours spent on different activities, etc.).

* Brainstorm creative ways to represent this data.

## Part 2: Data Collection and Conceptualization (30 minutes)

### Data Collection (15 minutes):
* Each group collects data relevant to their chosen theme. This can be done through quick surveys among classmates, personal tracking, or using pre-existing datasets.

* What aspect of the data do you want to highlight?

### Conceptualization (15 minutes):
* Groups discuss how to translate their data into a physical or performance art piece.

* Sketch or outline the concept.

## Part 3: Creation (45 minutes)

### Artwork/Performance Preparation (45 minutes):
* Create your artwork or prepare their performance.

* This can include drawing, crafting, rehearsing a skit, composing a piece of music, or choreographing a dance.

* We actively encourage creativity and unique interpretations of data.

Check out your colleagues results!

## Examples to inspire you if you are stuck!

* Observe and record classroom behaviors like questions asked or notes taken; represent with sketches or a simple bar graph.

* Inventory items in students' bags and create a pie chart using colored paper for each item type.

* Listen to and categorize different sounds in or around the classroom; create an abstract sound map using symbols or colored dots.

* Track movements in a specific area and represent the data through a dance or movement sequence.

* Note predominant colors in the classroom or from a window and create a collage matching these colors.

* Chart you and your fellow student;s current mood on a scale and compile this into a group artwork, using different colors or textures for each mood.

* Count the number of social interactions during a break and represent with string art, using different colors or lengths for each interaction type.

* Measure sound levels in different parts of the classroom and depict the data using a gradient of colors or heights in a sculpture.

* Identify light sources visible from the classroom and create artwork using transparent materials to represent each source.

* Record types of birds, trees, or natural elements seen from the classroom and create a nature-inspired piece of art using natural materials or drawings.
