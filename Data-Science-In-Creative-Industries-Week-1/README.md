## Unit 

This course uses programming approaches to introduce founding principles of data science to students. These include data gathering, data representation, data structures, and the difference between data and information. The course develops student’s understanding of statistical data mining approaches to analysis of structured and unstructured data, including common problems and challenges, such as issues with data distributions, data formats, and data bias. Furthermore, students will develop knowledge and skills in computer programming for gathering, processing and analysing data, including essential algorithms and frameworks. The course also discusses approaches to exploratory data analysis and visualisation techniques and machine learning models for recommendation systems and personalisation.

Students are also required to develop a robust understanding data storytelling and translating insights into impactful narratives for highlighting underrepresented voices, considering all knowledge within a historical, geographical, and cultural framework. We will also cover sentiment analysis and trend forecasting for creative industries.

### Learning Outcomes of Unit 

* **LO1** Explore and visualise creative industry data to identify patterns and trends 
(Communication).
* **LO2** Demonstrate fundamental skills in computer programming for gathering, 
processing and analysing data, including essential algorithms and frameworks 
(Process, Realisation)
* **LO3** Critically evaluate the impact of using data in creative practice in terms of social, 
racial and climate justice (Enquiry)

## Learning Outcomes of the Class

* Distinguish quantitative (discrete, continuous) from qualitative (nominal, ordinal) data, and structured from unstructured data, using creative industries examples (LO1)

* Load and explore a real tabular dataset with pandas: inspect types, summarise, select, count, sort, filter and find missing values (LO2)

* Critically discuss how datasets are made, whose experiences they record or erase, and why some datasets are missing altogether (LO3)

* Represent self-collected data through a hand-made or performed artefact, making deliberate choices about what to highlight (LO1)
