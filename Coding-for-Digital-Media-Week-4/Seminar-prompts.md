Pick one of these five prompts to discuss in your group 

## Artistic Intent vs. Legal Reality
The chapter describes how Bill Buchanan and Dickie Goodman's "The Flying Saucer" (1956) was deemed a parody and protected from copyright infringement, while many contemporary musician's face lawsuits. What factors determine whether sampling is legally acceptable? How do the different intentions behind sampling—parody, artistic experimentation, commercial production—affect both the creative process and legal outcomes?

## Technology and Accessibility
The Tape-beatles deliberately chose to work with "home stereo equipment" as part of their "egalitarian effort," while earlier avant-garde composers like Cage and Stockhausen required access to professional studios. How has the democratisation of sampling technology changed who can participate in sound collage? What are the advantages and disadvantages of this accessibility for both creators and the broader music culture?

## Cultural Context and Musical Borrowing
The text discusses how African American musical traditions, Jamaican reggae culture, and Brazilian Tropicália all embraced musical borrowing but for different cultural reasons—from oral tradition necessities to anti-assimilationist resistance to "cannibalist" aesthetic strategies. Compare these different cultural motivations for sampling. How do these contexts challenge Western concepts of originality and copyright?

## High Art vs. Popular Music
Scanner observes that visual artists "can simply get away with a lot more in terms of reference points than you can with sound" and that "lawyers seem to be a lot faster" to pursue commercial musicians. Why might sampling face more legal scrutiny in popular music than appropriation does in visual art or avant-garde composition? What role does commercialisation play in these different standards?

## Recognition and Transformation
Compare John Oswald's "plunderphonics" approach (which deliberately uses recognisable segments to create "confusion, disorientation, or cognitive dissonance") with Jamaican dub's "versioning" practice (which transforms originals into new works). What are the different artistic goals of these approaches? How might the degree of transformation or recognisability of source material affect both the artistic impact and the legal/ethical considerations of sampling?
